How a trusted employee photo enables Face Check
  1. 1HR confirms there is an approved hire.
  2. 2HR supplies an approved employee photo. An identity-proofing provider is optional when policy also requires government-ID assurance.
  3. 3{{ organization_name }} issues the trusted photo and employee claims in a signed credential.
  4. 4When requested, Microsoft Entra Face Check compares a live Authenticator capture to that photo, checks liveness, and returns only the match result.
How this prevents forgery

The identity validator establishes who the person is. {{ organization_name }} then issues a cryptographically signed credential to that person's wallet. A forged or edited credential fails signature validation because the attacker cannot reproduce the issuer's signing proof.

During presentation, the app verifies the trusted issuer, credential integrity, and the credential reference and UPN for this exact onboarding record. The immutable Entra object ID stays only in server state. A copied document, altered claim, counterfeit card, or credential from another employee cannot unlock the Temporary Access Pass.

Demo boundary: Face Check is available for credentials newly issued with an HR-approved photo after the tenant add-on and updated credential contract are enabled. Older credentials continue to support claim-only presentation.

Terminology: Microsoft Entra Face Check performs live selfie-to-credential-photo matching and liveness. A proofing provider separately validates government identity when that assurance is required.

Later use: onboarding or the help desk can request the issued credential alone or add Face Check for a fresh live match. The verifier receives a confidence result, not the selfie footage.

Important limitation: after acceptance, the credential is bound to the wallet's private key and cannot be forged by copying the card or editing its claims. The exposed point is before acceptance: this demo displays the issuance QR and PIN together, so anyone who obtains both could claim the real credential in their wallet.

Production control: bind a short-lived, single-use issuance request to the same authenticated device session that completed identity proofing, deliver the PIN through a separately verified channel, or require the wallet to present a proofing-provider credential before {{ organization_name }} issues its employee credential.

Why use Verified ID after manual proofing: a person or provider establishes identity once. The signed credential turns that decision into portable, tamper-evident evidence that later systems can verify consistently without repeating document review or trusting email, screenshots, and personal knowledge.
