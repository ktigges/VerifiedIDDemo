The identity-verification problem

Knowing personal information is not proof of identity.

Organizations often ask for a name, Social Security number, date of birth, employee number, security answer, or one-time code. Those checks can confirm what someone knows or receives, but not reliably who is presenting it.

What Verified ID changesA trusted issuer signs identity claims once. The employee holds that tamper-evident credential and presents the minimum required claims with consent for onboarding, help-desk verification, or recovery.

Why today's checks fail

  1. 1
    Knowledge is transferable

    Names, SSNs, birthdays, and employee details can be stolen, purchased, or socially engineered.

  2. 2
    Codes prove channel access

    An emailed or texted code may reach a compromised mailbox, SIM, or device.

  3. 3
    Manual review varies

    Agents make inconsistent decisions and handle more sensitive information than the request needs.

How decentralized identity works

Trust moves as signed evidence, not as a copied identity record.

The issuer signs claims with a key tied to its decentralized identifier (DID). The wallet holds the credential and its own key. During presentation, the verifier checks the issuer signature, holder proof, credential status, request state, and required claims.

Two different face controls

Face ID can unlock the wallet locally; the verifier receives no biometric result. Microsoft Entra Face Check is a premium Verified ID feature that compares a live Authenticator capture with a trusted photo claim in the credential and performs liveness. Microsoft returns the verifier a confidence result, not the selfie. A third-party provider is needed only for independent government-ID proofing, not for Face Check itself.

Customer choice

Self-issue what the customer can authoritatively attest. Buy proofing they cannot.

Build

Customer-operated issuer and verifier

  • Best for: employment status and employee identifiers sourced from HR.
  • Pros: control of claims, policy, integration, branding, and operating data.
  • Tradeoffs: customer owns secure issuance, wallet handoff, callbacks, revocation, monitoring, privacy, and support.
  • Limit: self-asserting a face match does not create independent identity assurance.
Buy

Identity-proofing provider

  • Best for: government-ID validation, document coverage, independent identity assurance, fraud signals, and recovery when every registered method and wallet are unavailable.
  • Pros: specialized controls, geographic/document support, and maintained proofing capabilities.
  • Tradeoffs: licensing, vendor dependency, data-processing review, user handoffs, and provider-specific assurance.
  • Limit: a provider is not required for Entra Face Check; the customer still owns the source-photo trust, HR matching, recovery policy, and final access decision.
